# Frequently asked questions

## Is this a course, a job, or a club?

A programme. It is structured — there are labs, in sequence, with material that
builds — but it is not a degree, it does not carry credit unless your institution
has separately arranged it, and it is not employment. Nobody is paid and nobody
is graded in the way a university grades.

## Do I have to be a student?

No. Students and working professionals both take part. The material assumes
technical competence, not a particular job title or stage of career.

## What will it cost me?

No fee. Your own time, and whatever your internet connection costs. Nothing needs to be
installed or downloaded — see below.

## What do I need on my machine?

Very little. Your code runs on our lab server; your machine only edits and
displays it.

- **macOS or Windows 11.** Both ship the SSH client this needs.
- **[Visual Studio Code](https://code.visualstudio.com/)**, free.
- An internet connection that can reach SSH.

No Python, no market data, no special hardware — an ordinary laptop is fine, and
nothing large is downloaded to it. Detailed setup instructions come after you
join; nothing needs installing before then.

## Do I need to know Python?

You need to be able to read it and change it. You will not be asked to build a
system from nothing — the framework is provided and you work inside it. If you
have never written Python, learn the basics first; this programme will not teach
them.

## Do I need to know anything about trading?

No. The trading concepts — mean reversion, momentum, pair trading, parameter
optimisation — are taught as part of the labs.

## Will I be trading real money?

No. The labs run against historical and simulated data.

## Who can see that I joined?

Your organisation membership is private by default — it does not show on your
GitHub profile unless you make it public yourself.

Your join request is a public issue, so your GitHub username is visible there.
That is why the form asks for nothing else. Once you are invited, the request is
closed and everything further happens in private repositories.

## Do I work alone or in a team?

In a team, sharing one repository. You still have your own GitHub account and
your own commit history — the account is yours, the work is the team's.

If you want to take part with a friend or colleague, say so and we will try to
place you together. Each of you should open your own join request.
